CERT-In just put a name to the AI attacks hitting Indian banks: CRAFTY FALCON.

On November 4, CERT-In and the I4C flagged an ongoing campaign using AI-assisted tools, ARTEX, PentAGI and CyberStrikeAI among them, originally built for legitimate security testing. These tools now hunt for weak payment APIs and misconfigured servers across banks, NBFCs, fintechs and crypto exchanges.

The pattern repeats every time: a Re 1 test transfer to see if it slips through undetected, then real money moving fast through mule accounts via IMPS and NEFT. The campaign even times its biggest moves for weekends and holidays, when monitoring thins out.

None of this is conceptually new. What's new is the speed AI brings to finding the gap and draining it before anyone notices.

CERT-In's advisories (CIAD-2026-0046 and CIAD-2026-0049) tell banks to set daily transfer limits, verify beneficiaries, flag unusually fast transactions and separate test environments from live systems.

For you: turn on transaction alerts so you see every debit immediately. If you spot a tiny test transfer you didn't make, don't wait, report it to your bank and to the national cybercrime helpline 1930 right away.
